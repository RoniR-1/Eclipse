# Submission and judging

## Published event rules

The 2026 event runs at EPFL on November 21–22. Teams can have up to four people. The event FAQ lists students, apprentices, PhD students and people who graduated within the last 24 months, aged 18 or older. The rules give organizers discretion over exceptions and exclude LauzHack members and committee members of partner associations. Check your roster against both sources. [Event schedule and FAQ](https://lauzhack.com/) · [Organizer rules, sections 19–23](https://lauzhack.com/pdf/rules.pdf)

The published Sunday schedule ends hacking at noon and lists judging from 12:30 to 15:00 in Lausanne. Teams submit and present, identify the work created for the event, and acknowledge existing resources. The rules describe overall and sponsor prizes and permit multiple awards. [Event schedule](https://lauzhack.com/) · [Judging rules, sections 28–34](https://lauzhack.com/pdf/rules.pdf)

## Confirm at the organizer desk before submitting

| Owner | Decision to obtain | Record before upload |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Submitter | Official platform, challenge listing, registration and exact upload cutoff | Submission URL, date, local time and time zone |
| Presenter | Pitch length, Q&A, live versus recorded demo, presentation language and slide or video requirements | Format and allotted minutes |
| Team lead | Registered team, recent-graduate or other exceptions, sponsor-specific eligibility and awards | Accepted roster and applicable challenge rules |
| Repository owner | Code visibility, access for judges, permitted dependencies, required deliverables and rights to any organizer material | Required access and written permission where applicable |

The published hacking end and judging window serve different purposes from an upload deadline or an individual pitch slot. Record the actual instructions from the organizer desk or participant announcements. The challenge rubric is a planning aid. [Demo and submission guide](../evaluation/demo-and-submission.md)
