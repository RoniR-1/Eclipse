## Formula and metric audit

The two interfaces answer different questions. `/api/simulate` returns branchable monthly nodes with detailed state and a compact timeline. `/api/project` starts every named path from one profile and returns monthly rows, path metrics, baseline comparisons, assumptions and sensitivity bands. A capability in one interface does not automatically reach callers of the other. [Simulation response](financial_twin/server.py) · [Projection response](financial_twin/projection.py)

| Documented formula or measure | Current implementation and test evidence | Gap or qualification |
|---|---|---|
| **Financial state and time.** Discrete \(t=0,\dots,T\). State covers cash, investments, debt, income, expenses, housing, goals, location/tax, employment/education, preferences and other circumstances. General transition \(x_{t+1}=F(x_t,a_t,w_t;\theta)\), event transition \(F_e\), observation \(o_t=H(x_t,d_t)\). | Both models step monthly. Engine nodes carry dates, parent IDs, balances, flows and active assumptions. A serialized checkpoint reproduces the uninterrupted path, including month-end dates. [Checkpoint test](tests/test_engine.py) | Housing, goals and employment context are richer in projection. Jurisdiction, observed-versus-simulated status and external-condition histories have no common representation across both APIs. The observation equation is a conceptual reference. |
| **Cash and savings.** \(CF=Y_{net}+O_{in}-E-T-DS-IC-K+IW\). \(C'=C+CF+R_{cash}=C+\sum CF_j\) when all flows are included. Simplified \(S'=S+Y-E\), or \(S'=S(1+r)+A-W\). | Engine deducts expenses, one-offs, actual debt payments and investment contributions. Projection deducts recurring costs, one-offs, debt payments and deposits. Tests check monthly reconciliation, all three starter paths and historical ledger replay. [Engine flows](financial_twin/engine.py) · [Projection recurrence](financial_twin/projection.py) · [Ledger test](tests/test_projection.py) | Cash interest, separately classified additional taxes, investment withdrawals and general transfer/inflow categories lack a common contract. Projection’s `net_cash_flow_chf` excludes locked deposits, so **closing cash = opening cash + net cash flow − deposit**. Using that field alone to explain the cash change gives the wrong answer in a deposit month. |
| **Investment holdings and compounding.** \(q'_k=q_k+b_k-s_k\), \(I=\sum q_kP_k\), \(I'=I(1+r)+A-W-F\). Constant return gives \(FV=PV(1+r)^n+A((1+r)^n-1)/r\), with \(FV=PV+nA\) at zero return. | Both apply effective monthly growth \((1+r_{annual})^{1/12}-1\). Engine grows opening holdings before making an end-period contribution. Its transfer and −100% asset-return test checks cash, holdings effects and net worth. Projection compounds opening investments. [Engine test](tests/test_engine.py) · [Projection recurrence](financial_twin/projection.py) | Holdings are CHF values. Neither interface maintains asset quantities, trade prices, sales or explicit fees. Projection lacks recurring investment contributions. A closed-form annuity check would independently validate timing and the zero-rate limit. |
| **Debt and fixed-term amortization.** \(D'=D(1+i)+B-P\). Fixed payment \(A=D_0i/[1-(1+i)^{-n}]\), or \(D_0/n\) at zero interest. | Both accrue \(i=\mathrm{APR}/12\), cap payment at the amount owed, and carry forward unpaid interest. Tests assert a positive-rate payment and balance. The loader preserves the scheduled payment on the source liability. [Engine test](tests/test_engine.py) · [Projection test](tests/test_projection.py) · [Loader](financial_twin/loaders.py) | Neither derives a payment from a term. The balance called `principal_chf` can include capitalized interest. Outputs distinguish accrued interest and total payment, but lack an explicit interest-paid, principal-paid, capitalized-interest and fee breakdown. A branch can introduce a liability without posting corresponding cash proceeds, so the funding side of a borrowing event needs an explicit treatment. [Branch change](financial_twin/server.py) |
| **Net worth and housing.** \(NW=A-L=C+I+H+O-D-L_{other}\). Transfers preserve wealth before fees, taxes or market moves. Housing examples include down payment, transaction costs, interest, amortization, maintenance, insurance, taxes, valuation, affordability and rent. | Engine sums cash and valued assets less debts. Projection sums cash, investments and locked deposits less debt. Tests verify that a rental deposit reduces liquidity while preserving its asset value, and that purchase fees and down payment post separately. [Deposit and purchase tests](tests/test_projection.py) | Projection represents the down payment as a locked deposit. Monthly ownership cost is `price × [(1 − down-payment share) × mortgage rate + maintenance share] / 12`. The purchase creates neither a gross property asset nor a mortgage balance. Initial net equity can look reasonable while reported debt and debt-service affordability omit the mortgage. There is no subsequent valuation, amortization or deposit-release schedule. [Housing calculation](financial_twin/projection.py) |
| **Inflation and purchasing power.** \(cost_t=cost_0(1+\pi)^t\), \(CPI_t=CPI_0(1+\pi)^t\), \(V_{real}=V_{nom}CPI_0/CPI_t\). | Engine compounds expense inflation monthly, supports calendar-year overrides and explicit expense exemptions, and preserves accumulated price level across a fork. Tests cover carry-forward, overrides and fixed-duration terms. [Engine test](tests/test_engine.py) · [Override tests](tests/test_web.py) | `/api/project` has no general inflation input. Neither API reports base-date purchasing power alongside nominal cash or net worth. Debt APR and effective annual investment/inflation rates also use different monthly conversions, a convention worth exposing. |
| **Liquidity, budget, debt service and savings rate.** The reference gives \(C_t\ge C_{min}\), \(DS_t/Y_t\le d_{max}\), and \(E_t+DS_t+IC_t\le Y_t+C_t^{available}\). Examples request minimum liquidity, monthly surplus, funding shortfall and goal feasibility. | Engine reports `max(0, −cash)` and caps investment contributions using an expense-based reserve. Projection reports minimum cash and date, first negative close, months with negative closes, average monthly net flow, runway and savings rate. Its runway is `max(cash, 0) / next recurring expenses`. Its savings rate is `sum(net_cash_flow) / sum(income)`. Zero denominators return `null`. [Allocation rule](financial_twin/engine.py) · [Projection metrics](financial_twin/projection.py) · [Shortfall test](tests/test_projection.py) | The allocation reserve restricts investing but does not flag a breached user minimum. Neither API returns the full set of threshold checks, severity and dates. Runway excludes debt service and one-offs. Projection’s savings rate excludes locked deposits and includes debt payments. Engine `savings_rate` is an **input preference** for surplus allocation. The docs do not prescribe one runway or savings-rate denominator. |
| **Goals and deadlines.** \(t_{goal}\le T_{goal}\), progress toward target, feasibility, and competition for the same resources. | Projection evaluates cash at the target date, reserves fully met targets in priority order, caps progress at 100%, and returns an unknown result beyond the horizon. A regression checks competition at a shared deadline. [Goal test](tests/test_projection.py) | It does not report first attainment, amount still needed or sustained affordability. Priority is evaluated before date, so a later high-priority goal can reduce the cash attributed to an earlier goal. That is a reservation policy worth showing. Engine branch nodes have no equivalent goal results. |
| **Counterfactuals and staged decisions.** \(\Delta x_T=x_T^{alt}-x_T^{base}\), \(\Delta M_T=M(x_T^{alt})-M(x_T^{base})\). Combined decisions must account for overlap. | Projection compares terminal cash and net worth, deepest cash difference and sustained net-worth break-even. It supports up to five alternatives with ten dated events each. Education, employment gaps, relocation, housing, shocks and custom changes affect underlying flows. Engine and server support nested forks and edits. Tests verify education break-even, sibling isolation and nested shocks. [Comparison](financial_twin/projection.py) · [Education and fork tests](tests/test_projection.py) · [Nested fork tests](tests/test_web.py) | Named projection paths start at the same root and have no shared-prefix tree output. Overlapping events apply in date order, with input order breaking ties. A server fork replaces the future phase plan and discards scheduled one-offs from the fork onward. This is explicitly tested and matters when comparing a single changed decision. [Fork contract](tests/test_web.py) |
| **Sensitivity and uncertainty.** \(M=f(\theta)\), sensitivity \(\partial M/\partial\theta_i\) or finite differences, and alternative parameter sets. Optional Monte Carlo draws \(w^{(k)}\sim P(w)\), simulates each path, and computes \(\bar M=N^{-1}\sum M^{(k)}\), quantiles, shortfall frequency or tail risk. | Projection runs deterministic optimistic and pessimistic paths by moving eligible directional, non-user assumptions together by ±15%. Tests show fixed user inputs stay fixed. Engine exposes investment share and a CHF volatility exposure equal to the sum of asset value times stated volatility. [Band test](tests/test_projection.py) · [Risk fields](financial_twin/engine.py) | Bands do not identify which assumption drove a result. They have no probability or percentile interpretation. The volatility sum has no covariance model. There is no one-at-a-time sensitivity table, sampled distribution, tail statistic or simulated shortfall frequency. |
| **Optional decision methods.** Discounted utility \(\max\sum_t\beta^tu(x_t,a_t)\), weighted objectives \(\max_a\sum_m\lambda_m f_m(a)\), Pareto frontiers, RL model \((S,A,P,R,\gamma)\), and the causal distinction \(P(Y\mid X)\ne P(Y\mid do(X))\). | The product implements deterministic conditional comparisons. Its preferences affect allocation and its local narrative reads calculated node values. [Narrative](financial_twin/server.py) | No optimizer, learned policy, causal estimator or quantum benchmark is evidenced in the audited paths. These are optional designs. Their respective validation rules concern feasibility, disclosed objectives/weights or rewards, baseline strategies, robustness, causal assumptions and classical benchmarks. ## Phase 1 edge cases and validation findings

- **CHF 0 scheduled debt payment.** The same one-month case was run through both recurrences. CHF 1,200 at 12% APR with a CHF 0 payment accrued **CHF 12**, closed at **CHF 1,212**, and took **CHF 0** from cash. This is especially important because all 283 supplied liabilities have a zero scheduled payment and are opening snapshots. Historical replay should use the supplied cash ledger. Forward capitalization and any optional term-based repayment are distinct projection assumptions. The existing loader test checks debt growth over 12 months. An exact zero-payment assertion and a payment-below-interest assertion would strengthen it. [Phase 1 findings](PHASE_1_AUDIT.md) · [Loader regression](tests/test_loaders.py)
- **Negative cash before payday.** A projection with CHF 100 opening cash, a CHF 500 upfront charge and CHF 1,000 income that month returns **CHF −400 minimum cash**, **CHF 600 closing cash**, `first_negative_month = null` and `months_in_shortfall = 0`. All four values follow the implementation. The latter two count negative **closes**. A user still needs CHF 400 under the model’s upfront-payment timing. There is also no automatic borrowing or liquidation in this case. [Monthly low point and shortfall metrics](financial_twin/projection.py)
- **Provenance and observations.** Projection tags profile and event fields, preserves user overrides and can return cached or researched event estimates with source URLs. Tests exercise source precedence, citations and missing-input defaults. The loader validates snapshot date and currency and excludes the cash asset already represented by opening cash. It does not carry per-field file, row, units and transformation history into engine nodes. Debt defaults, branch adjustments and projection goals likewise lack full field-level lineage. This matters for Alexia because **CHF 2,500 starter tuition, CHF 4,800 cohort median and CHF 8,000 profile-specific tuition** are three distinct annual assumptions. The 600 proposed, unexecuted events should remain outside historical replay. [Projection provenance](financial_twin/projection.py) · [Resolver tests](tests/test_event_resolver.py) · [Phase 1 source comparison](PHASE_1_AUDIT.md)
- **Units, boundaries and isolation.** Direct evidence covers zero-income career-gap months, negative investment returns, cash-to-asset transfers, debt accounting, missing/invalid fields, expense inflation, calendar timing, isolated branches and deterministic checkpoint continuation. The engine rejects non-CHF scenarios. There is no FX conversion convention, rate date or source for a multi-currency scenario. Net income is explicit in projection, while the branch engine’s generic income field relies on the caller’s convention. Useful additional assertions are early payoff, exact zero-rate amortization, higher tuition with all other inputs held fixed, zero-income debt-service ratios and coincident event ordering. Reproducible API examples should pin the start month and resolved assumptions because projection otherwise uses the current month and event estimates can refresh. [Validation checklist](ai-financial-twin-lauzhack-2026/evaluation/technical-validation.md) · [Input tests](tests/test_projection.py) · [Projection entry point](financial_twin/server.py)
- **Evidence for judges.** The examples request a starting profile, intervention, comparable baseline, assumptions, calculated differences, constraints, uncertainty, validation and limitations. Existing responses supply most numerical ingredients, including timelines, event markers and deterministic bands. There is no single replayable comparison response spanning the two interfaces with the jurisdiction, source dates, constraint outcomes, limitations and validation result together. The example JSON is illustrative. For stochastic work the checklist additionally asks for distributions, dependencies, seeds, sample counts and percentile meaning. For AI or game actions it asks for agreement with engine calculations and explicit constraint handling. The draft rubric also calls for accessible explanations, synthetic or licensed data and proportionate claims. ## Ranked upgrade plan

The **draft** rubric weights financial correctness **25**, scenario exploration **20**, technical validation **15**, communication **15**, innovation **15** and responsible design **10**. Each category earns `weight × score / 4`. The ordering below reflects which changes would improve the demonstrated financial decision and its evidence. [Judging rubric](ai-financial-twin-lauzhack-2026/evaluation/judging-rubric.md)

| Rank | Proposed incremental change | Acceptance evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Expose funding feasibility and cash reconciliation.** Add a cash-change-after-transfers field, first funding-gap period, maximum funding need, minimum-reserve breaches, budget breaches and debt-service ratio. Identify each threshold’s source. Keep existing closing-shortfall and savings-rate fields with their current meanings. | The CHF 400 pre-payday case reports its gap and positive close. A deposit month reconciles to the cent. Positive debt service at zero income has an explicit undefined ratio and affordability status. The starter master’s comparison shows its CHF 18,600 interim requirement alongside terminal cash. |
| **2** | **Make debt and housing auditable.** Add an opt-in fixed-term payment calculation and per-loan payment breakdown. Give financed purchases explicit property and mortgage balances, and identify the proceeds or asset received for new borrowing. Introduce detailed housing accounting through an explicit mode so current purchase outputs retain their contract. | Test CHF 0 payment, payment below interest, zero APR, early payoff, final-payment cap and no negative residual. On purchase, the initial asset, cash contribution, fees and mortgage reconcile. Later principal payments reduce cash and debt together. |
| **3** | **Expose purchasing power and assumption lineage.** Add base-month CPI and real cash/net worth, with explicit inflation coverage for recurring costs, fixed contracts and one-offs. Carry resolved value, source, as-of date, unit, frequency and transformation into a replayable scenario record where available. | Zero inflation reproduces current values. Twelve months at 2% produces a 1.02 price factor. A year-rate change preserves accumulated CPI. Alexia’s tuition can be traced to one specific source, and replay with frozen assumptions reproduces the result. |
| **4** | **Make staged decisions and goal commitments inspectable.** Expose event precedence, inherited and replaced commitments at a fork, goal cash reserved, first attainment, deadline gap and the reason a goal fails. Preserve the tested future-replacement fork behavior as the default. | A study → relocation → employment example identifies every overlapping salary and expense change. Sibling branches stay isolated. Same-date events, an earlier low-priority goal, a later high-priority goal and an out-of-horizon deadline have explicit results. |
| **5** | **Add attributable sensitivity and a judge-facing comparison.** Rerun one assumption at a time and show the change in terminal cash, minimum liquidity and goal outcome. Assemble one reproducible Alexia comparison with inputs, assumptions, events, conditional results, limitations and a visible sanity check. | Raising tuition alone cannot improve cash under otherwise identical assumptions. The comparison identifies the changed parameter, its unit, the perturbation and the resulting CHF difference. Existing combined bands remain independently reproducible. |

For each approved increment, the regression gate would be **all 52 current offline passes**, plus focused tests for the new behavior and the two database checks when a test database is available. Phase 2 stops here pending approval to edit code.
